How The Blend Works

How Value Is Calculated

First, the honest part: the app does not invent a point system. The big number on the right of every row is FantasyCalc's market value — what players actually cost in thousands of real, completed trades, re-queried for this league's exact format (dynasty or redraft, superflex or 1QB, team count, PPR setting). It is a market price, not a projection and not a score somebody assigned. Everything below is what this app does on top of that number, and each step is listed so you can disagree with it.

1 · TE premium adjustment. No market feed prices a TE-premium league. A starting tight end catches roughly 70 balls, so a +0.5/reception bonus is about 35 extra points a season. The app bumps TE values by 1 + (bonus × 0.36) — about +18% at +0.5/rec. This is an explicit approximation, not a measurement, and it is labelled as one everywhere it appears.

2 · Overall board order. Sorted by market value, highest first, because it is the only signal that compares a running back to a quarterback honestly. Ties break toward the better consensus rank. If the market feed is down, the board falls back to expert consensus weighted by position (superflex pushes QBs up sharply, K and DST fall near zero) and says so at the top rather than quietly showing you stale numbers.

3 · Consensus positional rank. The average of a player's positional rank across every source that covers him — the expert outlets plus the market's own positional rank. The "n src" on each row is how many sources that average is built from. Fewer sources means a softer number.

4 · The flags, and their exact thresholds.

SPLIT — at least 3 sources cover him, and the gap between his best and worst rank is at least 6 places and in the widest fifth of disagreements at his position. Deliberately relative: a 10-place spread means something different at QB than at WR.

BUY LOW / HYPED — the gap between where the market ranks him and where the analysts do. 6+ places of daylight in your favour flags BUY LOW; 6+ the other way flags HYPED.

RISING / FALLING — 30-day movement in real trade prices of 15 points or more.

5 · Tiers. Taken from the expert blend's own tier label where one exists, then the market's tier, and only if neither exists from positional rank (top 5 / 12 / 24 / 40). Tier is capped at 1–5.

6 · Draft picks. Priced by the same market as players — FantasyCalc quotes future rookie picks like "2027 1st" directly. A pick with no market price is left off the board entirely rather than given an invented value. In dynasty leagues picks count toward your total roster value, because a total that ignored them would flatter you.

7 · Trade packages get a depth discount. You can only start so many players, so the pieces in a package are worth progressively less: the best piece counts fully, the second at 92%, the third at about 85%, the fourth at 78%. Both the raw sum and the discounted total are shown on every trade so you can see exactly what the discount did. A deal inside 8% is called fair.

8 · Trade Ideas are scored on mutual benefit. Each proposal takes the smaller of what it does for you and what it does for the other manager. A lopsided steal scores zero and never appears, however good it looks for you — the point is offers that actually get accepted. Ideas must also land within 12% on value and fix a real positional hole on both rosters.

None of this predicts the future. It collapses the informed opinion that already exists into one screen and marks where that opinion is genuinely divided rather than merely loud.
